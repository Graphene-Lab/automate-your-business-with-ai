# Appendix E — Simple ROI Calculation

ROI tells you, in one number, whether a project paid for itself. You do not need a finance degree. This appendix gives the formula in plain words, a worked example, and a blank worksheet.

## The formula in plain words

> **ROI (%) = (Benefit − Cost) ÷ Cost × 100**

- **Benefit** = the value you gain, in money, over a period (usually one year).
- **Cost** = everything you spend, over the same period.
- A **positive** ROI means you gained. A **negative** ROI means you lost.

A ROI of 100% means you got back double what you spent. A ROI of 50% means you got back half again on top of your cost.

## What counts as Benefit

Put a money value on each gain you can defend:

- **Time saved** — hours saved × the hourly cost of the person doing it.
- **Errors avoided** — cost of rework, refunds, or fines you no longer pay.
- **Extra sales** — new revenue the change brings.
- **Faster response** — value of happier, retained customers (estimate carefully).

Only count gains you can explain. Do not pad the number.

## What counts as Cost

Add it all up:

- Software subscriptions (per year).
- Setup and integration work.
- Training time for staff.
- Ongoing human review of the AI's output.
- Any extra hardware or storage.

## Worked example

A small firm automates reading incoming invoices.

**Cost (per year):**
- Software: €1,200
- Setup: €800
- Training: €400
- Human review (2 hrs/week × €25): €2,600
- **Total cost = €5,000**

**Benefit (per year):**
- 40 invoices/week × 12 min saved = 8 hours/week saved
- 8 hrs × 50 weeks × €25/hr = **€10,000** in time saved
- Fewer errors (estimated): €1,000
- **Total benefit = €11,000**

**ROI = (11,000 − 5,000) ÷ 5,000 × 100 = 120%**

The project returns €1.20 of value for every €1 spent, on top of getting the cost back.

## Payback period

How long until gains cover the cost?

> **Payback (months) = Total cost ÷ (Benefit per year ÷ 12)**

Using the example: €5,000 ÷ (€11,000 ÷ 12) = €5,000 ÷ €917 ≈ **5.5 months**.

A short payback makes a project easy to approve.

## Second example (quick)

A support team uses AI to draft replies to common questions.

- **Cost/year:** software €2,400 + training €600 + review €3,000 = **€6,000**
- **Benefit/year:** 60 emails/week × 4 min saved = 4 hrs/week × 50 × €25 = **€5,000**, plus faster response value €2,000 = **€7,000**
- **ROI = (7,000 − 6,000) ÷ 6,000 × 100 ≈ 17%** — thin. The tool only pays off if review time drops or volume rises.

This shows why you must include the cost of human review. Skip it, and the ROI looks far better than it is.

## Non-financial benefits

Some gains are real but hard to price. Note them separately; do not force them into the number:

- Less boring work, happier staff.
- Faster response, better customer feeling.
- Fewer late nights and less stress.
- Consistency in how work is done.

Mention these in your case, but keep the ROI number honest.

## Honest tips

- Be conservative. If you are unsure of a benefit, lower it or drop it.
- Always include the cost of human review — AI still needs checking.
- A small positive ROI on a low-risk pilot is a good result.
- If ROI is negative, the idea may still be worth it for quality or compliance — say so plainly.
- Re-measure after the pilot with real numbers, not the estimates.
