# Appendix J — KPI Dashboard

A dashboard turns your AI projects into numbers you can watch. Numbers tell you if a change is working, stalling, or costing too much. Fill this in monthly. Keep it on one page. Share it with the team.

**KPI** means "Key Performance Indicator" — a number that shows how well something is going. Pick a few. Do not track everything.

## How to use this

1. Pick 5–8 KPIs that matter to your business.
2. Set a **target** for each (what "good" looks like).
3. Record the **current value** each period.
4. Name one **owner** per KPI — the person who keeps it accurate.
5. Review monthly. A KPI with no owner drifts and dies.

## Common automation KPIs

Use these to fill your rows. Each one is explained in plain words.

- **Time saved** — Hours the team no longer spends on a task. Measure: (old time − new time) × number of tasks.
- **Error rate** — How often the work comes out wrong. Measure: errors ÷ total tasks × 100. Lower is better.
- **Cost per task** — What one task costs now, all in. Measure: total cost ÷ number of tasks.
- **Customer satisfaction** — How happy customers are with the result. Measure: survey score, star rating, or simple 1–5.
- **Adoption rate** — How many staff actually use the tool. Measure: active users ÷ total users who should.
- **Response time** — How fast you reply or deliver. Measure: average time from request to answer.
- **Rework rate** — How often AI output must be redone by a human. Measure: redone tasks ÷ total tasks × 100.
- **Uptime / reliability** — How often the tool is available when needed. Measure: working hours ÷ total hours × 100.
- **Payback progress** — How close you are to earning back what you spent. See Appendix E.

## Example filled dashboard

| KPI | What it measures | Target | Current value | How often | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Time saved (invoices) | Hours not spent keying invoices | 30 hrs/month | 22 hrs/month | Monthly | Accounts clerk |
| Error rate (invoices) | Wrong invoice fields per 100 | < 2% | 3.5% | Weekly | Accounts clerk |
| Cost per task (support reply) | All-in cost to answer one ticket | < €1.50 | €1.20 | Monthly | Support lead |
| Customer satisfaction | Rating on resolved tickets | ≥ 4.5 / 5 | 4.2 / 5 | Monthly | Support lead |
| Adoption rate | Staff using the tool weekly | ≥ 80% | 61% | Monthly | Team manager |
| Rework rate | AI drafts needing a full redo | < 10% | 14% | Weekly | Team manager |
| Response time | Hours to first reply | < 4 hrs | 6 hrs | Weekly | Support lead |

## Reading the dashboard

- **Green** = at or better than target. Keep going.
- **Amber** = close but drifting. Watch it, adjust next period.
- **Red** = far off target. Investigate now or change the plan.

In the example above, time saved and cost look good. But error rate, adoption, and rework are red. That tells the owner: the tool works, but people are not using it well, and too many drafts need fixing. The fix is training and a review step, not a new tool.

## Rules for honest KPIs

- Measure the **real** value, not the hoped-for value.
- Use the **same method** every period, or the trend is meaningless.
- A KPI you never look at is a wasted KPI. Keep the list short.
- Tie every KPI to a decision. Ask: "What will I do differently if this number moves?"
- Review as a team. Numbers hide arguments; talking uncovers them.
