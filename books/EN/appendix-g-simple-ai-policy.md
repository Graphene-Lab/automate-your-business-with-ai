# Appendix G — A Simple AI Policy for Your Company

A short policy you can adapt and publish today. It tells staff what they may and may not do with AI, in plain words. Keep it to one page. Replace the bracketed text with your details.

---

**[Company Name] — AI Use Policy**
**Version 1.0 — Effective [date] — Owner: [name]**

## 1. Purpose

This policy sets simple, clear rules for using AI tools at work. It protects our customers, our data, and our reputation. It applies to everyone: employees, contractors, and anyone working for us.

## 2. Allowed uses

You may use approved AI tools to:

- Draft, summarise, or rewrite text (emails, reports, notes).
- Brainstorm ideas and options.
- Automate boring, repetitive tasks that follow clear rules.
- Help translate or clean up data.
- Speed up work that a human still checks before use.

Only use tools on the **Approved Tools List** (maintained by [name]).

## 3. Forbidden uses

You must **not**:

- Put customer personal or sensitive data into an unapproved AI tool.
- Paste confidential company data (financials, contracts, strategy) into public AI tools.
- Let AI make a final decision about a person (hiring, firing, credit, pricing) without a human deciding.
- Present AI output as your own finished work where accuracy matters, without checking it.
- Use AI to deceive, mislead, or impersonate a real person.
- Hide your use of AI when it matters that people know.

## 4. Data rules

- Check the **Data Inventory** before feeding data to any AI tool.
- **Sensitive data** (health, ID, bank, beliefs, children's data) never goes into an unapproved tool.
- Prefer tools that promise **not** to train their model on your data.
- Share the least data needed. Remove names and details if they are not required.
- When unsure whether data is safe to use, **do not use it** — ask first.

## 5. Disclosure

- If a customer interacts with AI (for example, a chatbot), tell them plainly.
- If AI wrote something important that a person relies on, a named human must review and own it.
- Follow any legal duty to disclose automated decisions.

## 6. Human review

- A human is responsible for every important output. AI assists; it does not decide.
- For anything that affects a person's rights, money, or safety, a human must check and approve.
- If you cannot check whether the AI is right, do not use its answer.

## 7. Third-party and shadow AI

- Use only tools on the Approved Tools List.
- Do not use personal AI accounts for company work.
- If you find a useful tool not on the list, request it — do not start using it silently.
- Using unapproved tools with company data is a policy breach.

## 8. If something goes wrong

- Report any mistake, leak, or odd AI behaviour to [name / email] the same day.
- Do not delete evidence. Save what happened.
- Report suspected prompt injection or suspicious instructions found inside documents.

## 9. Roles and responsibilities

- **Everyone** — Follow this policy. Ask when unsure.
- **Managers** — Make sure their team uses only approved tools and reviews output.
- **Policy owner ([name])** — Keeps the policy current, approves tools, answers questions.
- **IT / security ([name])** — Checks tools for safety, controls access, monitors incidents.

## 10. Exceptions

If you need to do something this policy does not allow, ask the policy owner first. Exceptions must be agreed in writing and are limited to a specific task and time. Do not assume an exception applies to you without it being granted.

## 11. Who to ask

- **Policy owner:** [name], [email]
- **Security / data questions:** [name], [email]
- **Tool approval requests:** [name], [email]

When in doubt, ask before you act. Asking is always the safe choice.
