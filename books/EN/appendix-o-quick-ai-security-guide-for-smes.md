# Appendix O — Quick AI Security Guide for SMBs

A small business using AI faces the same attacks as a big one, with fewer defenses. This is a fast, action-first checklist. Do the top items first. Each one closes a real door. Work down the list as time allows.

**The core idea:** Treat AI tools like a new employee with access to your files. Give the least access needed, watch what they do, and know what to do if something goes wrong.

## 1. Accounts & access

- [ ] Every AI tool login uses a **strong, unique password** (not reused from anywhere else).
- [ ] **Multi-factor authentication (MFA)** is on for every AI account. This is the single best fix.
- [ ] Each person has **their own login** — no shared "team" accounts.
- [ ] Access is **per person and per need** — not everyone sees every tool or every data set.
- [ ] Ex-staff and changed roles have access **removed the same day**.
- [ ] Admin rights are held by **as few people as possible**.
- [ ] You keep a **list of who has access** to what, and review it monthly.

## 2. Data handling

- [ ] You know **what data** each AI tool can see and store.
- [ ] **Personal and sensitive data** (customer details, health, finance, IDs) is never pasted into public AI tools.
- [ ] You have an **approved-tools-only list** and staff know it by heart.
- [ ] Data is **encrypted** both stored and in transit (ask the vendor to confirm).
- [ ] You **minimise** what you feed the AI — only the data the task needs, nothing extra.
- [ ] You can **delete** your data from a tool and get proof it is gone.
- [ ] Backups of your own data exist and are **not** dependent only on the AI vendor.

## 3. Vendor checks

- [ ] You read the vendor's **privacy and security terms** before signing.
- [ ] You confirmed your data is **not used to train** their shared model (or you agreed to it in writing).
- [ ] You know **where** your data is stored (which country/region).
- [ ] The vendor has a **breach-notification promise** — how fast they tell you.
- [ ] You checked the vendor's **reputation** (reviews, references, how long they have operated).
- [ ] You know their **sub-processors** (other companies touching your data).
- [ ] You can **export your data** and leave without a trap.

## 4. Staff & shadow AI

- [ ] Staff know **which tools are approved** and which are banned on company devices.
- [ ] You have talked about **shadow AI** — staff using unapproved tools behind your back.
- [ ] Staff know **never to paste** customer personal data into a public chatbot.
- [ ] Staff know AI output **must be checked** before it goes to a customer or a payment.
- [ ] There is a **simple way to ask** "is this tool okay?" before someone signs up.
- [ ] New staff get a **short AI-safety briefing** on day one.

## 5. Monitoring

- [ ] You keep an **audit log** of who used which AI tool and did what.
- [ ] You **review logs** regularly, not only after a disaster.
- [ ] You set **usage and cost alerts** so a surprise bill does not appear.
- [ ] You **spot-check AI output** on a schedule to catch drift or errors early.
- [ ] You watch for **unusual access** — odd hours, odd locations, bulk downloads.

## 6. Incident basics (when something goes wrong)

- [ ] You have **one named person** in charge if an AI incident happens.
- [ ] You know the **first three moves**: stop the tool, contain the data, tell the owner.
- [ ] You can **cut off access fast** — revoke logins, pause the tool, block the account.
- [ ] You know **who to notify**: the vendor, your data-protection contact, and (if required) the regulator.
- [ ] You keep a **written record** of what happened and what you did.
- [ ] You have a **plain message ready** for customers if their data was involved.
- [ ] After the incident, you **fix the cause**, not just the symptom.

## The 10 items that matter most

If you do nothing else, do these today:

1. Turn on **MFA** everywhere.
2. Ban pasting **personal data** into public AI tools.
3. Make an **approved-tools-only list**.
4. Give **least access** — per person, per need.
5. Remove access for **ex-staff** the same day.
6. Confirm your data is **not training** the vendor's shared model.
7. Keep an **audit log** and look at it.
8. Require **human review** before AI output reaches a customer or payment.
9. Set **cost alerts** so the bill cannot surprise you.
10. Name an **incident owner** and write the first three moves.

## Golden rules

- **Least access always.** Give the minimum needed, nothing more.
- **Trust, but check.** Review logs and outputs; do not assume.
- **Data you never send is data you never leak.** Feed the AI the minimum.
- **A plan you have written is faster than a plan you invent under stress.**

Security is not one product you buy. It is a set of small habits you keep. Start with the ten items above and build from there.
