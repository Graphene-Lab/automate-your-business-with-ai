# Appendix C — Template to Map Your Processes

Before you automate anything, write down what you actually do. This template captures one row per process. Fill it for your top 5–10 repeated tasks. You cannot improve what you have not mapped.

## How to use it

1. List the tasks your team repeats every week.
2. For each one, fill a row below. Be honest about time and pain.
3. Mark **Automation potential**: High (boring, rule-based, high volume), Medium (partly automatic), Low (needs human judgement).
4. Start with High-potential, high-pain rows.

## Column guide

- **Process name** — A short, clear label anyone would understand.
- **Owner** — The single person responsible for this process today.
- **Steps** — Numbered actions, in order. Keep each step to one action.
- **Inputs** — What goes in: documents, emails, forms, data.
- **Outputs** — What comes out: a record, a reply, a file, a decision.
- **Time per run** — Real minutes it takes, not what you wish it took.
- **Frequency** — How often it happens per day or week.
- **Pain points** — What goes wrong, what is slow, what people dislike.
- **Automation potential** — High / Medium / Low, with a reason if not obvious.

## Tips for good mapping

- Map the process **as it really is**, not as the manual says.
- Ask the person who does the work, not the person who manages it.
- Note every "copy-paste" step — those are prime automation targets.
- If a step needs trust or judgement, mark it for human review.
- Total the time: `time per run × frequency` shows where the hours go each week.

## High-potential processes to look for

These repeat often and follow clear rules — good first targets:

- Reading and sorting incoming email or documents.
- Copying data from one system to another.
- Answering the same customer questions over and over.
- Drafting standard replies, quotes, or reports.
- Checking documents for missing or wrong fields.
- Summarising long notes, calls, or files.

Avoid automating first: anything that needs trust, negotiation, or a final judgement about a person.

## Quick priority grid

Sort your rows to pick the first thing to automate.

| | Low pain | High pain |
|---|---|---|
| **High potential** | Good later | **Start here** |
| **Low potential** | Ignore | Fix the process first, not with AI |

Pick one "Start here" row for your first pilot. Keep it small.

## How to estimate time honestly

- Time the task once or twice for real; do not guess from memory.
- Include the hidden minutes: finding the file, switching screens, fixing mistakes.
- Ask two people who do the task and compare their numbers.
- Use a normal week, not a quiet one.

## Using the map

- The map shows where the hours go each week — that is your target list.
- A process with high time and high pain is worth automating first.
- If a process is painful but low volume, fix the cause before adding tools.
- Keep the map alive: update it when a process changes. It is a working document, not a one-off.
