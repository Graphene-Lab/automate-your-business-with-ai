# Appendix I — Pilot Project Template

A pilot is a small, cheap trial that answers one question: does this work for us? Keep it narrow, time-boxed, and honest. This one-page plan holds everything you need. Fill it before you start, and judge the result against it at the end.

## How to use it

1. Pick **one** process (from your process map) with high potential and high pain.
2. Fill every field below. If you cannot fill it, the pilot is not ready.
3. Set a fixed end date and a clear decision rule.
4. Run it, measure, then decide: scale, change, or stop.

## Example snapshot (a finished pilot)

- **Goal:** Cut invoice processing from 12 to under 4 minutes, no extra errors.
- **Process:** Process incoming invoices (from process map).
- **Before:** 12 min/run, 40/week, 6% error rate.
- **After (target):** 3 min/run, 40/week, ≤6% error rate.
- **Success met?** Time 3.1 min ✓, errors 5% ✓, staff score 4 ✓, no incidents ✓.
- **Cost during pilot:** €600.
- **Decision:** Scale — all criteria met, no open High risk.
- **Next:** Roll out to all invoices; keep human review on amounts over €5,000.

## What to do after the pilot

- **If you scale:** plan the wider rollout, keep review where risk is high, set a review date.
- **If you retry:** write down what failed, change one thing, run a short new pilot.
- **If you stop:** record why, so the lesson is kept and not repeated.
- Share the result with the team either way. A failed pilot that taught something is a good use of a small budget.

## Pilot rules

- Keep it small: one process, one team, a few weeks.
- A human reviews every output during the pilot.
- Measure the baseline first — never skip this.
- A pilot that fails cheaply is a success: you learned without a big loss.
- Write the result down and share it, good or bad.
